# Phase 1: decision core, backtest, generated metrics

Target branch: `dev`
Scope: `ARCHITECTURE.md` 1.1 to 1.6. Phase 2 not started.
Code snapshot: [`pr/code/`](code/)

## What this does

Offline decision system for reviewing wrongly-blocked orders. Evidence
assembly, a calibrated model, an expected-value policy, a chronological
backtest, and a metrics harness that regenerates `METRICS.md` from a seed.

No LLM, no network calls, no non-determinism. The agent in Phase 2 imports
these modules unchanged, so every decision it makes is one the backtest has
already graded.

Headline on a 1,775-case temporal holdout: 98.6% overturn precision, 81.1%
recall of recoverable revenue, ₹7.04 cr recovered against ₹22.57 L of fraud
admitted, ₹1.53 cr net contribution, 17% abstention.

Read the accuracy section before quoting any of that.

## How valid is this

Answering this properly because the numbers above look better than they are.

### The rupee figures are seed noise

`python run.py seeds` reruns the whole pipeline on five independently generated
worlds:

| seed | holdout | local AUC | +network AUC | lift | precision | net contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 42 | 1775 | 0.8682 | 0.9134 | +0.0452 | 98.6% | ₹1.53 cr |
| 1 | 1745 | 0.8501 | 0.9209 | +0.0709 | 97.8% | ₹1.87 cr |
| 2 | 1486 | 0.8414 | 0.9066 | +0.0651 | 98.4% | ₹1.15 cr |
| 3 | 1826 | 0.8602 | 0.9321 | +0.0719 | 99.1% | ₹1.80 cr |
| 4 | 1800 | 0.8678 | 0.9109 | +0.0431 | 98.5% | ₹1.19 cr |

Net contribution ranges ₹1.15 cr to ₹1.87 cr, sd ₹30 L. That spread is wider
than the bootstrap interval reported in `METRICS.md` 1, which means the
limiting factor is how the world was generated, not how many cases are in the
holdout. Any single rupee figure is a point estimate from one draw.

Precision holds up: 97.8% to 99.1%. AUC lift ranges +0.043 to +0.072, mean
+0.059. Seed 42, used throughout, is the least favourable of the five.

Quote precision as a number. Quote money as a range.

### The confidence intervals were computed wrong, now fixed

36% of holdout cases belong to a customer appearing more than once. Those cases
share a network file, a device and a persona, so resampling cases
independently understates variance. `bootstrap_ci` now resamples customers.

The correction turned out to be small, 0.95x to 1.11x on interval width, but
the previous method was wrong on principle and would have been worse on a
dataset with heavier repeat structure.

### "Holdout scored once" was not true

Earlier drafts of `METRICS.md` claimed the holdout was scored once. It is read
many times: headline, four baselines, an 11-point frontier, per-merchant, three
ablations, two sensitivity grids, the failure exhibit.

What is true, and what the claim should have said, is that no parameter,
threshold or operating point was chosen on it. Those come from the calibration
slice. The line in `METRICS.md` now says that instead.

### Value is concentrated in one merchant

Aurum Jewels is 451 of 1,775 holdout cases and 77% of holdout value. The rupee
headline is largely a measurement of one merchant's blocked pile. Per-merchant
results are broken out in `METRICS.md` 3.

### The moat is smaller than our own docs claim, and I over-corrected once

`IDEA.md` 4.1 claims +0.107 AUC. Actual is +0.045 on seed 42, +0.059 averaged
over five seeds. The network model reproduces closely; the gap is that
local-only is stronger than documented, 0.868 against a claimed 0.813. The
shipped generator leaks more persona through merchant-visible signals than the
version those docs were written against.

Separately, an interim measurement during this work put the moat at +63% of net
recovery. That compared both models at a shared raw-probability threshold,
which measures calibration rather than evidence. Most of the apparent gap was
the local model's raw scores sitting on a different scale. Corrected in
`development/README.md`; `datagen/moat_ledger.py` keeps the naive comparison
so the difference between the two is inspectable.

What holds: network evidence releases 188 more orders at higher precision at
the same time. A threshold move trades those against each other. This is true
at every operating point measured, and does not depend on the size of the
effect.

### Remaining limits

- Ground truth is authored. Calibration and sweeps reduce circularity; they
  don't remove it.
- `network_clean_rate` carries 0.346 of gain at rank 1, against the ~0.49
  `DATA_CARD.md` 6.4 warns about. Confirmed, not resolved.
- Step-up pass rates are assumed. Swept across a 3x3 grid, never
  point-estimated.
- Fraudsters in this data don't adapt to the review system existing.
- Determinism is verified within one machine. Float behaviour across platforms
  is not tested.

### What I'd trust

The mechanism, the direction of the results, and precision. The rupee
magnitudes are a demonstration that the accounting works, not a forecast.

## Design decisions

**Answer key behind one module.** `core/truth.py` is the only thing that opens
`ground_truth.jsonl.gz`. `training_labels()` raises `HoldoutPeek` on a non-train
id; `grade()` is unrestricted and used by `metrics.py`. `test_leakage.py`
AST-parses `feature_store.py`, `policy.py` and `backtest.py` and asserts none of
them import it.

**Ablation is an argument, not a fork.** `as_matrix(cases, blocks=("local",))`
against `("local","network")`, asserted by a test, so both arms run the same
code.

**Calibration.** The policy consumes probabilities as prices, so isotonic
regression is fitted on the last 20% of train, chronologically. Model fitted on
the first 80%, never refitted. Hyperparameters are fixed constants: with ~6.5k
rows a search would fit the calibration slice.

**Three-way temporal split.** Fit on train[:80%], calibrate on train[80%:],
choose operating points on that same slice, report on holdout.

**Two rupee columns.** Net (gross convention) is recovered minus admitted, how
the industry and `IDEA.md` quote it. Net contribution is margin on recovered
sales minus the full basket plus overhead on bad releases, which is what
reaches the P&L and what the policy optimises. They diverge; release-everything
scores ₹5.51 cr on the first and −₹8.79 L on the second.

**Step-up outcomes are parametric.** The dataset can't say whether a customer
would pass verification, so `backtest.py` records `STEP_UP` unresolved and
`metrics.py` resolves it against a declared grid. This also keeps truth out of
`backtest.py`.

## Other findings

**The cap saturates at 0.20.** `EV > 0` requires `p < m/(1+m)` as `f/A` goes to
zero, independent of order size. At a 25% margin that is 0.20, so any cap above
it does nothing. Margin bounds how much doubt a release can carry, not risk
appetite. `IDEA.md` 7 sweeps a threshold to 0.50, which an EV policy can't
reach. Regenerate that table before using it.

**Five of six showcase cases are in the training split.** `IDEA.md` 5 narrates
six payment IDs. All six exist in the regenerated data with matching outcomes,
but only one is holdout, and the model is fitted on the first 80% of train.
`notebooks/04_worked_cases.ipynb` finds holdout substitutes for each narrative
role. Two of the six also no longer behave as written: Case 3 abstains where
the pitch says it overturns, Case 5 abstains rather than producing its ₹8.4 L
loss.

**LightGBM's default importance hid a known problem.** `DATA_CARD.md` 6.4 warns
that `network_clean_rate` dominates. LightGBM's default `feature_importances_`
is split count, which is flat and ranks it sixth at 0.085, making the warning
look resolved. Gain puts it first at 0.346. `model.importances()` now takes a
`kind` argument and `METRICS.md` 4 prints both.

**`IDEA.md` and `DATA_CARD.md` carry stale numbers throughout.** Row counts,
block rates and the moat table all come from an earlier generator. Audit in
`development/README.md`.

## Defect fixes

**`--n 300000` broke calibration.** `n_customers` was fixed at 12,000 and `--n`
never touched it, so regenerating at 300k tripled orders-per-customer from 8.3
to 25:

| | block rate | FP share | value ratio | holdout blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100k baseline | 2.82% | 75.5% | 3.79x | 622 |
| 300k, customers fixed | 6.31% | 84.2% | 5.09x | 5,374 |
| 300k, customers scaled | 2.76% | 76.8% | 4.09x | 1,775 |

Added `--customers`, defaulting to a value that scales with `--n`.

**Insufficiency gate could route a certain-fraud case toward release.**
`ARCHITECTURE.md` 1.3 runs the thin-file gate before the confidence check,
which sent a case scored at `p_bad = 1.000` into a step-up dialogue that a
passed verification could have released. Reordered so the confidence check runs
first: upholding needs no exculpatory evidence because the block is already in
place, so the gate only has to guard releases. Covered by
`test_upholding_needs_no_exculpatory_evidence`, with the "no confidence level
releases a thin file" invariant asserted separately.

## Tests

33 passed in 4s.

Leakage (9): AST check that quarantined modules never import the answer key;
training door raises on holdout ids; no answer-key columns reach the store; no
single feature scores above 0.99 AUC alone; shuffle test, where holdout AUC
collapses from 0.913 to 0.49 under permuted training labels; point-in-time
counters monotone per customer across 3,401 consecutive pairs; tenure can't
outgrow wall-clock; ablation is a flag not a fork.

Policy (12): EV strictly decreasing in `p_bad`; release propensity monotone at
five amounts; margin ceiling at three margins; no confidence level releases a
thin file; confidently-bad upholds a thin file; same `p_bad` different amounts
gives different actions; every decision carries reasons; only OVERTURN moves
money.

Determinism (10): two replays byte-identical; redecide at the same config is
identity; moving the operating point moves no probability; release count
monotone in cap; grading and step-up resolution deterministic; per-merchant
application preserves every case.

Against the Phase 1 definition of done: `METRICS.md` regenerates with one
command, yes. Holdout precision and recall with CIs, yes. Three test files
green, yes. Moat ablation at least +0.05 AUC: +0.045 on seed 42, so no on this
seed, and +0.059 averaged across five.
